# Consent, debrief and recruitment texts

The texts participants read, in English and Bahasa Indonesia, for the ethics submission. The consent
and debrief below are generated from `src/lib/study.ts` (`TEXTS` and `TEXTS_ID`) at version 3.0.0; if
either changes, change the other and regenerate this file. Square brackets mark what is still to be
filled in.

How the consent is given: the participant reads the text on the first screen of `/study` and ticks the
checkbox; the study cannot start without it. Nothing is stored before that. Age is self-declared in the
same checkbox.

How the deception is handled: the consent says that some details are explained at the end. The debrief,
shown to everyone who finishes, names each case whose advice was deliberately wrong and the wrong fact
the advisor was given, and offers to delete the answers of anyone who would rather they were not used now that they know. The final screen shows the participant ID that a deletion request needs.

## English

### Consent: Before you start

This is a research study about how people use investment advice from an automated advisor. It is run by the Department of Information Systems, Institut Teknologi Sepuluh Nopember (ITS), Surabaya, and takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

**What you will do.** You will read eight short stories about made-up investors. For each one, an automated advisor recommends how to invest, and you say whether you would follow the advice and how much you trust it. Before the stories there are three questions about money and some short statements about how you think. After them there are a few ratings and two questions you may leave empty.

**Good to know.** Nothing here is real financial advice and no real money is involved. Please answer as if you were the person in each story. Some details of the study are explained at the end.

**Who can take part.** Anyone aged 18 or older. Taking part is voluntary, and you can stop at any time by closing this page, without giving a reason.

**Your data.** We do not ask for your name, email or any account. Your answers are stored under a random participant ID, together with the advice you saw, so they cannot be traced back to you. If you stop part way, your progress is kept in this browser for up to a week so you can continue, and it is deleted when you finish.

**How the answers are used.** The anonymous answers are analysed for research papers and may be shared in a public research data archive, so that other researchers can check the results. Until the data are published, you can have your answers deleted by sending your participant ID to the researcher.

*While the study is a pilot:* This is a trial run (pilot) to test the study before the main data collection. Answers from the trial run are used to improve the study and are not reported as research results.

*Once approved, in place of the pilot note:* This study was approved by {committee} (reference {reference}).

Contact line: For questions, or to have your answers deleted later, write to Raditya Pratama (radityapratama2077@gmail.com) [or the supervising lecturer, NAME (EMAIL)]. The privacy page lists everything that is recorded.

Checkbox: ☐ I am 18 or older, I have read the information above, and I agree to take part.

### Debrief: Thank you. One more thing you should know

In some of the stories, the advisor was given one or two wrong facts about the person on purpose, so its advice did not suit them. We did this to see how people react to good and bad advice from an automated advisor. In these stories, the advice was based on a wrong fact:

- Story 4: Steady mid-life investor. The advisor was told a low attitude to risk instead of medium.
- (one line for each flawed case the participant saw)

Real automated advisors can make the same kind of mistake when information is entered or read wrongly, so it is always worth checking that advice fits your own situation. The advisor here is part of a research study, not a financial service. If, now that you know this, you would rather your answers were not used, send the participant ID on the next screen to the researcher and they will be deleted.
